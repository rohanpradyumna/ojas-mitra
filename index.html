the maker

A promise kept in a courtyard.

I began this from my own kitchen, in a small village in south India.

at home, ’26

I began Ojas Mithra in the courtyard of my own home. At forty-six, I have spent more than half my life among families and farms across south India — a climate activist by training, a community psychologist by listening, learning what people put in their hands and their water, and what they wish they could put instead.

Before my mother passed, she asked me to make something that could reach Indian homes at scale — quietly, kindly, without harm. This is that promise, in bottles.

I fill every order from my backyard. The recipes use bio-enzymes — fruit waste fermented for three months in clay pots — and plant extracts gathered from the trees and gardens around me. Nothing leaves the kitchen until it has been on the shelf long enough that my family and I have used it ourselves.

— with care, Kavita

on the shelf

Seven jars, kept the way we’d keep them at home.

No ranges. No bestsellers. Each one earns its place.

i.
Dishwash
Tough on grease. Gentle on hands. Smells faintly of lemon and something older.
 ii.
Hand wash
For hands that have been outside, or in the kitchen, or both. Aloe and tulsi.
 iii.
Toilet cleaner
Thick enough to cling, gentle enough for septic tanks. No fumes that climb the stairs.
 iv.
Floor cleaner
Neem, lemon, eucalyptus. The smell of a house just after the morning mop.
 v.
Vegetables & fruits
For what comes off the cart, the field, or someone’s hands. Soak, rinse, eat.
 vi.
Laundry liquid
Low foam, for buckets and machines alike. Colours remember themselves.
 vii.
Kumkumadhi Tailam
An old Ayurvedic oil for the face. Saffron, sandalwood, patience.
