our way

A letter about how this is made.

We are a small family enterprise in a village in Andhra Pradesh. The workshop is the back half of our house. The supply room is the room next to the kitchen. The clay pots in which we ferment the citrus peels sit in the shade of the courtyard, beside the tulsi and the curry-leaf tree.

We started because Kavita’s mother, before she passed in 2022, asked her to do one more thing. She had spent a lifetime watching neighbours struggle with what they put in their water, on their floors, on their skin — bottles of chemicals their grandmothers wouldn’t have recognised, all priced affordably and sold without much thought. She knew her daughter could do better. Make something for ordinary homes, she said. Make it so it is theirs.

So we did, slowly, while figuring out how. Kavita is a climate activist and a community psychologist; the work is hers, but it sits on top of a much larger one — the work that families in our part of India have always done to make do, to make clean, to make safe, without much help.

We do not make anything we would not pour into our own bucket, our own sink, our own hands.

The bottles in our catalogue are bio-enzyme based. That means the cleaning power comes from a quiet liquid we ferment from citrus peels — the same peels we used to throw away, and which our grandmothers would have known what to do with. They go into clay pots with jaggery and water, and they sit for ninety days in the shade. What comes out at the end is gently acidic, mildly cleansing, and alive with the kind of enzymes that food waste produces when given time. We blend this base with plant surfactants, essential oils, and small amounts of well-chosen things like neem and aloe.

We do not test on animals. We do not use synthetic fragrance, synthetic colour, parabens, sulphates, optical brighteners, or anything that ends in a number we don’t recognise. We list every ingredient in plain words on the label and on the page. If you want to know more, write to us.

The Kumkumadhi Tailam is the odd one out. It belongs to a different tradition entirely — the slow oil-based tailams of classical Ayurveda. We make it the way the old books describe, in small batches, in sesame oil, with saffron and sandalwood and fifteen other things. It is the bottle we are proudest of, and the one we make least of, because it takes the longest.

The recipes are old. The pace is slow. The promise is plain.

We pack everything ourselves. Every order is filled by hand, here, by Kavita or by one of the two women who help her — both from the same village, both paid the way we would want our daughters to be paid. We use cotton instead of bubble wrap. We re-use cardboard. We accept the bottle back when it is empty and refill it for less, if you are near enough to bring it.

We have a WhatsApp number. We do not have a checkout, because we like to know who we are sending bottles to. Some orders take two days; some take a week, if Kavita is travelling for community work. We will tell you. We will answer.

We are not in a hurry. We hope you aren’t either.
