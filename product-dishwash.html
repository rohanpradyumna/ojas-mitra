Dishwash

Tough on grease. Gentle on the hands that wash up after every meal. बर्तन धोना

This is the bottle that sits beside the kitchen sink. It is what I use to wash the kadhai after the day's cooking, the steel plates after dinner, and the glass jars after the chutney has been finished. It does not foam excessively — that is decorative, not useful — but it cuts grease the way a hot cloth and patience would, and it does not leave the hands raw.

The fragrance is lemon and a faint note of lemongrass. It is the smell of a kitchen being put to bed for the night.

What is inside

  • Bio-enzymes (citrus peel, jaggery, water, fermented 90 days)
  • Saponified coconut oil
  • Cold-pressed lemon extract
  • Lemongrass essential oil
  • Tulsi leaf extract
  • Aloe vera juice
  • Plant-derived foaming agent (decyl glucoside)
  • Spring water from a borewell on the property

The bio-enzyme note

I collect citrus peels from my own kitchen and the neighbours’. They go into a clay pot with jaggery and water and rest for three months in the shade of the courtyard. The result is a quiet, working liquid that cleans without harshness. This is the base of most of what I make.

How to use it

  • One small squeeze in half a basin of warm water is enough.
  • Keeps for nine months. Use it before that and I will be glad.
  • Refills available — bring back the bottle when it is empty.
  • Stored away from direct sun, it will be patient.
